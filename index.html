Independent Research, 2026 (In Progress)

Can Machine Learning Improve a Multi-Factor Long–Short Equity Signal? An Out-of-Sample Comparison After Costs

Status: Walk-forward experiments running

Built on my multi-factor backtesting framework (see Projects).

QuestionCan ML (gradient boosting / random forest) combine momentum, value, and quality into a better long–short signal than an equal-weight linear composite — out-of-sample, US large-caps, after costs?

DesignPre-registered null hypothesis: ML does not beat the linear composite OOS after costs. Baseline comes from my backtesting framework, where the equal-weight composite delivers ~0 OOS Sharpe after costs. Same universe, same 8 bps cost model, walk-forward training with purge/embargo, judged on Sharpe, deflated Sharpe, drawdown, and turnover.

Deliverable6–8 page paper (PDF) plus a public repo; the ML combiner is a small addition on top of the existing backtesting engine.

The honest, pre-registered design is the point, not a placeholder for it. No results are posted here until the walk-forward experiments finish — including if the null hypothesis holds.

Quant Research

Factor Backtester Flagship

Built a Python research pipeline for long-short S&P 500 factor strategies using momentum, value, and quality signals. The project emphasizes point-in-time universe construction, delisted-name handling, realistic trading costs, walk-forward validation, and deflated Sharpe analysis.

2010-2024 test window 8 bps per-trade cost model 62 network-mocked tests 0.02 out-of-sample Sharpe

The result was intentionally not over-marketed: the tested naive factor mix did not produce meaningful risk-adjusted returns after costs. That is the point of the work: build research infrastructure that can reject weak ideas instead of dressing them up.
